Five places work can pile up

Simplified: SwiftUI may combine, skip or schedule this work differently.

Not equivalent

When NPay feels slow, different kinds of work could be responsible. Click through the stages: application state, where large updates or expensive computations happen; view evaluation, where SwiftUI re-runs the bodies that depend on what changed; layout, where views measure and position their content; rendering, drawing and compositing; and display, frames reaching the screen.

SAY

A display refreshing at 60 hertz has about 16.67 milliseconds between refresh opportunities: a thousand divided by sixty. At 120 hertz, 8.33. The text’s chart includes 90 hertz, 11.11. These are reference intervals, not guaranteed CPU budgets for your SwiftUI code.

SAY

Click: if the application does too much work during a critical update, frames miss their presentation deadlines and the user sees hitches: our quarter-second gap on the last slide is about fifteen 60-hertz intervals.

SAY

Lesson 3.11 · Recap

What to remember

Lazy views, not lazy data
VStack built 10,000 rows; LazyVStack 15; List 9

Stable ids, narrow reads, light bodies
no UUID() per access; no big sort per keystroke

Measure, and free the main actor
0 frames while it was busy; observe → measure → change one thing

SAY

Three things to remember. Lazy containers create views for the visible region: an eager VStack built all ten thousand rows, LazyVStack fifteen and List nine; but the data itself is still all in memory, which pagination solves later. Keep identity stable and work out of body: a computed UUID made every row new, and a sort in body ran on every keystroke. And measure: counts, Instruments, one change at a time, remembering that a busy main actor delivers no frames however lazy the list is.
